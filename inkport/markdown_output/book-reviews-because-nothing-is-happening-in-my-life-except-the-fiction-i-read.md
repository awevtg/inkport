# Book reviews, because nothing is happening in my life except the fiction I read.

*By samayra — Thu, 09 Jul 2026 05:08:57 GMT*

**Tags:** `writing`, `book-review`, `memories`, `journaling`, `reading`

In last week’s article, I said I really didn’t have anything to write. In the piece I wrote months ago, I said when I don’t write, I read, and here I am after reading four books in six days. And… I am also going to stop justifying the actions I take from now on because it’s my article you have decided to read, my account you have stumbled upon, and my words you are reading with your own voice in your head, with or without Spotify. If not, I recommend playing “Blowing Smoke” by Gracie Abrams, which is on repeat in my ears (in both earbuds) while writing this.

First and foremost, with some context, after reading Heaven by Mieko Kawakami… I took a break. I published a piece about it on Medium , then my life went on a breakdance. I, well, was lagging in one way or glitching in another. Life had no structure. I was filling things out, including my name and choices like backpacks, clothes, utilities, and colleges, so I wouldn’t end up in a ditch somewhere after four years. I was on Google Maps the most, then mostly in my emails for any updates. Sometimes the dates got extended, and I, well, was living in this hamster wheel for a long while, I believe. But at last, I secured my backup option, and what I want is still in progress, so I am quite stable now. I don’t believe it when I say this.

So, yes, we are talking about book reviews. The four books I read included The Vegetarian, Deep Cuts, Lie with Me, and Yesteryear.

When you see the chronological order of reading, you could perfectly assume that I was on a surfboard, balancing with my hands open to the world, dependent on the wind, on tides of emotions that were trying to delve me deeper into a realm I could no longer break through. Did I come out? I don’t know yet, but I feel like I am suspended there, like Billie Eilish on the cover of Hit Me Hard and Soft.

The Vegetarian was recommended by the algorithm itself after I added my review of Heaven.

It was, like, everywhere. I have known of this book for quite some time before, the red-covered book which I always excused my reading self from because it would take too much time. Additionally, I believed I could finish it in a day. Then you spend the rest of the day convincing yourself you were productive, only to realise what a scaredy-cat you are.

The ultimate truth is, I am scared of books. Of those books, people say, changed the views they had before. That transformed their ideals, their characters in the theatre of life. Big words. I am gatekeeping Sylvia Plath for the same reason. Haven’t read The Bell Jar because my indecisive brain cells, or neurons, believe that they would be unemployed after reading it.

Like how I don’t want to get unemployed in the near future. I hate when I relate everything to myself.

The book starts with the character’s husband, then her brother-in-law (I wished he had just fallen off the balcony), and then her sister. Did I already have a soft spot for her? Why did I, at times, want her to do as they said? Why did I throw the book across the room three times? Why did I want to literally strangle the sister? Why did I relate when I read the same hatred for hospitals or food? Sometimes, I think I should have just said no. Why did I see my reflection so intensely after completing the book in the bathroom? Why didn’t I know about Mongolian birthmarks? I hate the book. Why did I read it now? Well, I just turned eighteen some time before.

I want to answer all these questions. I want to experience everything again. These are my wants, though. What I only need is just another book so that I will be out of this limbo because this book didn’t lead me to write anything but to appreciate everything a little more, sometimes a little less, and realise that I define my own standard of what ‘normal’ means.

The next book was Deep Cuts. Thought it was fluff. It turned me into fluff.

Had some other engagements involved in my life, so it took me two and a half days to complete. I don’t like it and don’t hate it; I will probably reread it. It was not the time for this book.

Lie with Me… man. I don’t think I have a first love yet. Too young, some say. Too old, some will say.

Though my longing was not for a lover, but for a destination.

What heartbreak truly is. Is it a smile on a boy’s face sitting on a grass field? Maybe it is. Maybe I want to believe that it is.

I love the book. I hate monologues. Death is too certain to ever become a choice. I don’t want to be a coward in love. I don’t ever want to be a fool in love.

The last book, this one, was not on my reading list for this month or even this year. Whenever I added a new book to my Goodreads list, this one was always in my recommendations, though I never added it. I already had twenty-one books waiting.

I was already thinking of rereading one of the Harry Potter books (Half-Blood Prince) to cleanse my palate as I was planning to dive into the classic, The Idiot, rather than Crime and Punishment.

But I failed to find my Half-Blood Prince copy. I found it later in the cardboard box where the rest of my school textbooks were. In there, I also found some notebooks. Notebooks with notes, scribbles, rough sheets, and some with a lot of pages left. When I opened one of them, the pristine blankness asked me a question I am afraid of putting into words here.

Yesteryear is among the few books in my life like what Normal People is. And that statement only makes sense to me.

Yesteryear builds different narratives not through people but through the points in time from which they are narrated. When I was trying to write fiction, I used a parallel structure in my storyline.

The main challenge was that I wasn’t fully able to comprehend the ending, even though I was the author of it. I couldn’t arrange the overlapping timelines in a way that made perfect sense to the reader. But Yesteryear did that incredibly well, and it made me envious of the author while reading it.

The story itself was like throwing a stone into a river and watching it skip across the surface. Knowing perfectly well that the next time you won’t be able to do it again, and even if you do, you’ll probably call it luck.

Am I quoting my life right now?

Maybe.

I am.
